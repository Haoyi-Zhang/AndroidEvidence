\section{Introduction}

Two Android packages may look alike for many legitimate and illegitimate reasons. They may be separate builds of one code base, applications that embed the same advertising library, products generated from one template, regional variants signed by different subsidiaries, siblings produced from the same prompt, or an original and an unauthorized derivative. A detector can be accurate about a shared feature while still being unable to distinguish among those histories. The central problem is therefore not merely whether two artifacts are similar. It is whether the observation is strong enough for the relationship asserted by an analyst, marketplace, developer, or incident responder.

Parts of this distinction are already explicit in Android security surveys: Xu et al. separate legitimate privacy disclosure from malicious leakage and organize ecosystem roles, while Tam et al. distinguish analysis observations, detection outcomes and dataset limits~\cite{xuandroid,tamandroid}. Repackaging studies examine code reuse and graph resemblance~\cite{droidmoss,attackclones}, advertising and watermark evidence~\cite{adrob,appink}, and malicious grafting~\cite{piggybacking}. Third-party-library (TPL) work examines common-library separation and detection~\cite{commonlibraries,libd,libradar}. Family and version identification must additionally handle shrinking, partial inclusion, and obfuscation~\cite{libscout,libid,atvhunter}. Software-supply-chain work separates authenticated updates and delegation~\cite{tuf,diplomat} from recorded build steps and publication histories~\cite{intoto,chainiac}. Its questions concern actors, consumed materials, produced artifacts, authorization and observer consistency. These bodies of work are complementary, but their guarantees do not compose automatically.

AI-mediated development makes the boundary more visible. A text-to-application system can generate template-based MIT App Inventor projects from natural-language descriptions, while Android development benchmarks evaluate generated projects and coding agents~\cite{text2app,appforge}. Assistant studies examine insecure suggestions and user interaction~\cite{pearce,perry,sandoval}, nonexistent package proposals~\cite{spracklen}, and sensitive-material reproduction~\cite{codexleaks}. The final APK alone does not record the causal history of iterative revisions. An application can therefore be novel at the byte level, similar at the behavioral or user-interface level, composed partly of inherited code, and produced through an authorized but weakly recorded process. No single binary relation captures all of those facts.

The practical consequence is a recurring category error. A high similarity score is reported as proof of copying; a component match is treated as proof of application ancestry; a valid signature is treated as proof of rightful authorization; an SBOM entry is treated as proof that the listed code is present and reachable; or a reproducible build is treated as proof that the inputs were approved. Each inference may become defensible with additional evidence, but the missing premise is often implicit. Once hidden, it is difficult to benchmark, transfer to a new workflow, or challenge during an appeal.

Prior synthesis is substantial: RePack reviewed 57 repackaging approaches and their comparison limits~\cite{repack}; Wu et al. survey detection techniques and datasets~\cite{wurepack2026}. Android TPL reviews cover module, family, version, maintenance and security questions~\cite{tpl,zengtpsurvey2024}. Supply-chain syntheses map attacks and design properties~\cite{astra,properties,ladisa}, complemented by research-direction and risk reviews~\cite{williams2025,cyberrisk2026} and empirical SBOM studies~\cite{bi2024sbom,bomsaway2024}. Our contribution is neither first coverage of these topics nor a general supply-chain taxonomy.

The question is claim-centered: \emph{which observations support Android lineage and assurance propositions under explicit transformation and trust assumptions, and which bindings compose artifact, component, build, release, behavior, and AI-mediated evidence?} Distinct evidence objects and counter-histories motivate it; the incomplete \ReviewMatrixWorks{}-record matrix cannot establish a literature gap.

The survey answers four research questions:

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1: Claim structure.] What distinct lineage and assurance claims recur in Android analysis, and which evidence objects can support each claim?
\item[RQ2: Transformation robustness.] How do rewriting, obfuscation, resource changes, library reuse, compilation, and generation alter the validity of artifact-level evidence?
\item[RQ3: Cross-layer composition.] How can artifact analysis, component evidence, authenticated process records, release authorization, and behavior analysis be combined without overstating any layer?
\item[RQ4: AI-mediated development.] Which assumptions become fragile when source and mobile artifacts are generated or revised through nondeterministic, tool-using systems?
\end{description}

Existing frameworks already separate supply-chain artifacts, operations, actors, properties, and defenses, and explicitly discuss AI development~\cite{properties,astra,williams2025}. Building on those distinctions, this review offers an Android-focused seven-level claim ladder; a mechanism map linking objects, transformations, oracles, ceilings, and counter-histories; a digest-bound cross-layer assurance pattern; and an analysis of AI-mediated sibling generation, synthesized dependencies, hidden context, tool state, and model drift. The ladder adds propositions rather than ranking every higher level as universally better.

The map retains \CorpusWorks{} records, including \PrimaryWorks{} primary/system studies. The reading ledger and unresolved coding are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:method}; the inventory supplies neither a complete search denominator nor independent dual coding.

The synthesis is that lineage evidence is \emph{non-substitutable}: artifact and component evidence recover relationships when process records are absent; authenticated records establish direction and accountability; release controls bind distribution authority; and behavior analysis addresses security properties. Composition strengthens assurance only when artifacts, principals, times, policies, and transformations are explicitly bound.

Section~\ref{sec:method} gives the review design. Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:ai} develop the claim model and domain syntheses; Sections~\ref{sec:synthesis} and~\ref{sec:agenda} derive the cross-layer blueprint and agenda.
